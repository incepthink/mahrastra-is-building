The brief

What we are building

The requirement was never simply to collect the news. It was to answer one question honestly: what is on fire right now, in which district, and can we prove it?

Most news dashboards show you volume — how many articles were published. That tells you very little, because volume is mostly a measure of how many journalists a paper employs. We are measuring something different: which stories are growing, how fast, and how far they have spread across the press and the public. Everything below follows from that one distinction.

Commitments

Three rules the whole system follows

These are the reasons certain decisions look the way they do. If you disagree with any of the three, tell us now — each one has shaped a large amount of what is already built.

Every number is something we watched happen

The ranking is arithmetic on counts we observed ourselves. “Eleven of thirteen outlets carried it, and it held a top-three slot for nine hours” is a claim we can defend. “The system ranked it highest” is not.

The map never claims more precision than we have

When we know the exact town, the story gets a sharp pin. When we only know the district, it gets a soft marker that visibly says so. A precise-looking pin placed on a guess is the worst thing a map like this can do.

AI names stories — it never ranks them

AI does exactly one job: reading a group of articles and saying what they are about, so coverage from different days can be joined into one story. It never decides what is important. Counting stays counting.

How it works

Five stages, in order

This is the sequence a single news article passes through, from the moment it is published to the moment it appears on the map.

1

Collect — every fifteen minutes, around the clock

An automated job sweeps the section pages of Marathi news sites and records two things for every story: how many pages carry it, and how high up each page it sits. Repeating this every fifteen minutes is what creates a history, and history is the only thing that makes growth measurable.

Today: two dailies (Lokmat and Loksatta) plus view counts from eight Marathi news YouTube channels. Running unattended since 30 August 2026.

2

Group — turn scattered articles into one story

This is two separate problems, and we solve them two different ways. Two papers covering the same event on the same day reuse the same words, so plain text comparison catches it reliably and costs nothing. The same story continuing across several days cannot be caught that way — each new article exists precisely because it says something new, so there is no shared wording to find. That is where the one AI step goes: it reads each group and returns what the story is about, and we join groups across days on that.

3

Locate — by what the article says, not by who filed it

This is done in code, not by AI. We match place names found in the article text against a list of all 356 talukas in Maharashtra, handling both Marathi and English spellings of the same name.

The trap this avoids is a serious one. Every article carries the newsroom desk that filed it, and it is tempting to place stories on the map using that. It would be wrong. A recent tribal students' march from Amravati to Yavatmal was filed under Nagpur three separate times — nothing happened in Nagpur, that is simply where the paper's regional desk sits. Using the desk would have put that one story on the map four times, three of them in the wrong place.

4

Rank — measure the change, not the size

A story that has been on every front page for a week is big. A story that went from one page to thirty in an hour is on fire. We already measure size; the ranking measures the change — how fast a story is spreading across pages, climbing up them, and drawing new coverage.

5

Present — three ways to read the same map

A heat view shades districts by how much is happening. A pin view marks individual stories in place. Clicking a pin opens the story as a step-by-step timeline rather than a list, because the interesting thing about a running story is that it moved — the march above travelled from Amravati to Babulgaon to the state secretariat over two days, and a list hides exactly that.

Growth signals

The four sources that tell us a story is spreading

You asked which services these are. There are four, and we are adding them cheapest-and-strongest first. The first is already running and costs nothing.

Live In build Planned
Our own press sweep Live

How many section pages carry a story and how high it sits, sampled every fifteen minutes. No external service, no API cost, no rate limit. This is the backbone of the ranking.

Google News RSS In build

Takes us from two mastheads to roughly thirty. How many separate outlets pick a story up — and how quickly they join — is the single strongest signal available to us, and it is free. This is the next thing we build.

YouTube In build

Views per hour on Marathi news channels — the only genuine measure of public attention rather than editorial choice. Collection is already running; matching each video to the right story is the remaining work.

Google Trends Planned

What the public is searching for, added last and used only to corroborate. Its numbers are rescaled on every request, so it can confirm a story is rising but cannot be trusted to compare two stories directly.

Second source

Government releases, and sharing them

Press releases from the state's information directorate (Mahasamvad) sit on the map as a second, separate source. Around 141 releases across 34 districts, pulled live rather than copied by hand, so the layer stays current on its own.

Two points worth agreeing on explicitly. First, this is a filter, not a third view. Heat and pins are two readings of the same press coverage, so they are mutually exclusive — but government releases are a different source, so the switch layers over either one. Second, we know each release's district with certainty, because the department files its own releases by district. Nothing is guessed here.

Selected releases can be sent to WhatsApp, the clipboard, or the phone's own share sheet, with the message previewed before it goes. The message carries the release's opening paragraph word for word — we never summarise or rewrite a government department's copy.

Also live

Minister filter

The map can be filtered to the Chief Minister, the Deputy Chief Ministers, cabinet ministers, ministers of state, or any single named minister — applying across the heat view, the pins, the side panel and the government layer together.

One thing to flag, because it needs an owner: the minister list is maintained by hand from the Gazette of 2 February 2026. Nothing updates it automatically, and nothing will warn us when it goes stale, so the date it was last checked is printed on the map for readers to see. A reshuffle means someone editing that file.

Open items

What we already know is imperfect

Stated plainly so nothing here surprises you in a demo.

Statewide stories can land on the wrong town

A story with no single location sometimes picks up a stray place name from its text and gets pinned there. It is visible on the map today and it is the first fix on the list.

Share links point at our development machine

Shared links are built from whatever address the site is served on. Until a production domain is confirmed, anything shared is unusable outside our office. We need that domain from you.

Mumbai is missing from the government taxonomy

The directorate files secretariat releases without a district, so Mumbai's releases come from a small separate set we maintain. Worth knowing before someone asks why Mumbai looks quiet.

History begins 30 August 2026

That is the honest left edge of every trend line on the map. Older articles exist, but the fifteen-minute record that growth is calculated from starts there, and comparisons cannot reach behind it.

Over to you

Five decisions we need from you

Each of these changes what we build next, so an answer on any of them is immediately useful.

Breadth or public attention — which matters more?

More outlets (Google News RSS) tells you how widely the press is carrying a story. YouTube and Trends tell you what the public is actually watching and searching. We recommend breadth first, as it is free and the stronger signal — but if public attention is the point of the product for you, we will reorder.

What is the production domain?

Needed before sharing works for anyone outside the team. This is currently the most blocking item on the list.

Who owns the minister list after a reshuffle?

It takes minutes to update but somebody has to notice. We can hold it, or hand you a simple procedure — we just need to agree which.

Should statewide stories appear on the map at all?

Stories with no single location currently have nowhere honest to sit. Our preference is to keep them out of the map and list them in a separate statewide strip, rather than place them somewhere arbitrary.

Is fifteen minutes the right cadence?

It is comfortable for current sources. Faster sharpens the growth curves and increases load; slower saves nothing meaningful. Happy to keep it unless you want tighter resolution.
