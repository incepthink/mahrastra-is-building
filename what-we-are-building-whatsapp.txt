*Maharashtra Is Building*

A live map of Maharashtra that answers one question:
*What is on fire right now, and where?*

*What we measure*
Which stories are _growing_ — spreading to more outlets, climbing higher up the page, pulling in more views — and which district they are actually happening in.

Big is not the same as on fire. A story on every front page for a week is big. A story that went from one page to thirty in an hour is on fire. We rank the second one.

*Where the news comes from*
• *Marathi press — live.* We sweep _Lokmat_ and _Loksatta_ every 15 minutes, recording how many section pages carry each story and how high it sits on them.
• *Government releases — live.* _Mahasamvad_ (mahasamvad.in, the state information directorate) is pulled live as its own layer — around 141 releases across 34 districts. Their district is never guessed: the department files its own releases by district.
• *Google News RSS — next.* Takes us from two mastheads to roughly thirty. How many separate outlets pick a story up, and how fast they join, is the strongest signal available to us — and it is free.

*How we rank*
• *Our own press sweep* is the backbone: pages carrying a story, position on each page, every 15 minutes. No API, no cost, no rate limit.
• *YouTube* gives the one thing newspapers cannot — a number the public generated rather than an editor. Views per hour across eight Marathi news channels: ABP Majha, TV9 Marathi, Zee 24 Taas, Saam TV, News18 Marathi, Lokmat, Mumbai Tak, Loksatta.
• *Google Trends* comes last, and only to corroborate. Its numbers are rescaled on every request, so it can confirm a story is rising but cannot compare two stories against each other.

*How it works, in five lines*
1. *Collect* — the 15-minute sweep. That repetition is what creates a history, and history is what makes growth measurable.
2. *Group* — scattered articles get joined into one story, including the same story continuing across several days.
3. *Locate* — we place a story by the place names _in the article text_, not by the desk that filed it. (A march from Amravati to Yavatmal was filed under Nagpur three times. Nothing happened in Nagpur.)
4. *Rank* — by how fast a story is spreading, not by how large it already is.
5. *Show* — heat view by district, pins for individual stories, and a click opens the story as a timeline so you can watch it move.

*Filtering the map*
Two filters, and they combine.
• *By time* — 24 hours, 48 hours, 7 days, 30 days, or all of it. 24 hours is the default. If a window is empty the map widens to the next one and says so, instead of showing a blank state and implying nothing happened.
• *By minister* — the Chief Minister, the Deputy Chief Ministers, cabinet ministers, ministers of state, or any single named minister — applied across the heat view, the pins, the side panel and the government layer at once.

Both live in the page's address, so every view of the map is a link somebody can send.

*Sharing a government release*
Open any Mahasamvad release on the map and send it straight to WhatsApp, the clipboard, or the phone's own share sheet — with the message previewed before it goes. The message carries the release's opening paragraph _word for word_. We never summarise or rewrite a government department's copy.

*Three rules we hold to*
• *Every number is something we watched happen.* "Eleven of thirteen outlets carried it, and it held a top-three slot for nine hours" is defensible. "The system ranked it highest" is not.
• *The map never fakes precision.* Exact town → sharp pin. District only → soft marker that visibly says so.
• *AI names stories, it never ranks them.* Its only job is reading a group of articles and saying what they are about. Counting stays counting.
